# Where Bombs Fell

*dust*

Geocached bronze roundels, audio and walking guide, 2016

## Summary

Bronze roundels set into Sheffield's streets, each marking a site bombed in the Blitz and telling a fragment of the story of the city.

## Description

Where Bombs Fell in Sheffield is a companion piece to the 1948 map of the same title. Created by Matthew Edgar for the Catalyst Festival of Creativity 2016, it marks key sites bombed during the Sheffield Blitz, connecting the dots to tell stories about the city either side of the Second World War.

Each location is marked with a bespoke 80mm bronze roundel, sand cast and set into the pavement. Every roundel carries the date, the place and the kind of bomb, around a line of its own: Dulce et Decorum Est Pro Patria Mori at Barker's Pool, Wonderland at Fitzalan Square, Wherever the River May Take Us at Pond Hill. The lettering is Granby, a 1930 typeface from Stephenson Blake, Sheffield's own type foundry, cut and stacked to fill the circle.

Each roundel's location was geocached, so the bronzes did two jobs: they mapped the sites, and they delivered audio content to the walker's phone on arrival. dust designed the identity, the roundels and a pocket walking guide printed in fluorescent pink, with a spread for each stop: the roundel's line set large, the history of the site, and directions to the next.

The roundels have weathered into the streets since, gathering verdigris and wear. The walks carried on, with music and readings to accompany them, including a sold-out Design Week walk run by the Graphic Design & Illustration department at Sheffield Hallam University in October 2017.

## Materials

- Bronze roundels, 80mm, sand cast
- Pocket walking guide, fluorescent pink
- Geocached locations, delivering audio to a phone app
