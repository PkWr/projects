# Concrete

*Live installation*

No Bounds Festival, 2025

## Summary

A running concrete mixer, staring back. What the eye sees in the drum is fed live into a kaleidoscope, turned over together and split into mirrored fragments that never become one. A piece about separation, segregation and the pull towards homogenisation, and what is at stake in them: culture, power, identity, kindness and empathy.

## Description

An industrial concrete mixer runs in the basement of Castle House. A camera mounted inside the drum transmits what it sees, live, into a kaleidoscope, where it is blended in real time with archival material and fragments of literary text.

The piece holds two opposite pressures. The mixer homogenises, turning everything over until it is one uniform mass. The kaleidoscope separates, cutting the same material into mirrored segments that are divided, repeated and held apart. Between them sit separation, segregation and the pull towards homogenisation, and what is at stake in each: culture, power, identity, kindness and empathy.

Concrete moves on from Working Backwards (2023). Where those pieces used generative systems hand-coded by the artist, Concrete was built with AI-assisted tooling, giving the live mix a mixing-desk range of parameters.

The set-up is a comment in itself. The whole visual mix runs on a Raspberry Pi, a credit-card-sized computer drawing roughly 3 to 9 watts, where the usual tools for live visuals, such as TouchDesigner or Resolume, need a machine with a dedicated graphics card drawing tens to hundreds of watts. There is no rendering pass either: rather than sequences rendered out in advance over hours and stored as large video files, each frame is generated only as it is projected, and nothing is kept.

AI plays a particular part in that. It was used to build the tool, not to run it: its energy cost is spent once, in development, and the finished piece runs live with no model, no cloud service and no data centre behind it, on a few watts. That sits against the current discourse on AI and sustainability, where attention falls on the growing electricity and water demands of data centres. Here AI is used as a source of efficiency, and managing that trade-off becomes part of the work.

Against that, the machinery dominates the room. The mixer's motor draws around 500 watts, and the projector more on top, so the computer is the smallest load by far. Planning around that power, and running real machinery safely in a festival space, became as much a part of the work as the code.

## Materials

- Industrial concrete mixer, running
- Camera mounted inside the drum
- Raspberry Pi running the live visual mix
- Projection
- Archival material and literary text, mixed in real time
